# Building a Workbench with Limited Tools

A 24" x 48" woodworking bench, 36" high, built from pre-cut 2x4 studs, plywood and OSB, with a lower shelf and a Harbor Freight DOYLE 7" carpenter's vise. It was designed for a friend with few tools, while travelling and without access to a usual shop.

**Tools assumed:** a 7-1/4" circular saw (a table saw was only a maybe), a drill/driver and an impact driver, a speed square, a 48" level (used as a saw guide), a jack plane, a handsaw, four 24" F-clamps, two sawhorses and a 48" x 48" plywood sheet as a temporary work surface. No bench to build it on, and no jigs.

The plans were developed step by step in a conversation with Claude (Anthropic), with the builders correcting them from what they found at the bench. See [ITERATIONS.md](ITERATIONS.md) for how each version came about and why.

## The design in brief

- **Top:** 23/32" plywood over 3/4" OSB, 24" x 48", glued and screwed together from the OSB side only.
- **Legs:** two 2x4s glued face to face. The inner board (A, 33-3/4") is full length. The outer board (F, 30-1/4") is 3-1/2" shorter, leaving a notch at the top for the upper rail. The rail is half-lapped into the notch.
- **Rails:** upper and shelf-level rails lap over the faces of the legs (face-grain joints, no end-grain screwing). The long rails run 1-1/2" past the legs to cover the ends of the end rails.
- **Shelf:** 3/4" OSB at 24" above the floor. The four 5" x 4-1/2" corner cut-outs from the shelf become the feet.
- **Vise:** between the legs at the front left. The front upper rail is notched 2-1/4" up from its bottom edge so its 1-1/4" strip and the 1-1/4" pad pieces share the vise mounting plane, 2-23/32" below the top surface.
- **Dog holes:** 3/4" holes 5" apart in line with the vise dog, with several shop-made dog designs.

## Materials and cost

8 pre-cut 92-5/8" 2x4 studs, one 4 x 8 sheet of 3/4" OSB, a 24" x 48" piece of 23/32" plywood, #10 x 3-1/2" and #10 x 2-1/2" screws, #8 x 1-1/4" screws, wood glue, four lag bolts, a handsaw. About **$144 with tax** at Menards (St. Paul / Roseville, MN, October 2026) when the plywood is a used or leftover piece, or about $200 with a new sheet. The vise was about $70 at Harbor Freight (2023 launch price). Prices change; confirm them. Details are on the last page of the Wide Base PDF.

## Open items

- The pad length (14") is provisional until the vise's rear bracket is measured.
- The notch length (11") is provisional until the casting width is measured.
- The dog hole row assumes the vise dog travels parallel to the front edge. Rotate it 90 degrees if it travels front to back.

## Using the plans

These are woodworking plans developed for one build. Check every dimension against your own materials (plywood and OSB thickness, the vise you buy) before cutting. Use at your own risk. You are welcome to use and adapt them; add a license file if you want to set terms.
