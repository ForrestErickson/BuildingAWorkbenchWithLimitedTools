## Key lessons that changed the design

1. **No end-grain screwing.** Screws into the end grain of 2x4s hold poorly, so every rail laps over the face of a leg (iteration 03 refined this into a half lap).
2. **Cover the end grain.** Extending B by 1-1/2" at each end hides the end of C and J (02).
3. **Feet from the shelf.** The corner cut-outs needed for the legs are the right size to be feet, so no hardware is needed (04).
4. **Thicknesses are measured, not assumed.** Leg length is 36" minus the top thickness minus the foot. The as-built plywood and OSB were 23/32" and 3/4" (06b).
5. **Where screws go matters.** Driving the top-layer screws from the plywood face leaves holes in the work surface, so the build sequence warns to drive them from the OSB side (06b).
6. **The vise sets the stack height.** The mounting plane is 2-23/32" below the top surface (23/32" + 3/4" + 1-1/4"). B is 3-1/2" tall, so 4-31/32" with the top; 2-1/4" must be cut from B (07).

## Open questions at the time of the final plan

- The pad length (14") and the notch length (11") are provisional until the vise's rear bracket and casting width are measured.
- Does the vise dog travel parallel to the front edge or front to back? The dog-hole row is drawn for parallel and can be rotated.
- The Narrow Base Bench plan has not been updated with the as-built improvements.
